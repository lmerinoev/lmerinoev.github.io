Resources

In a suggested order. Start with the two video series for Modules 1 and 2, then the two reinforcement learning sources, which the RLHF book assumes. Read the papers alongside the modules that cite them. Everything here is free to read or watch online except the 1952 paper at the end.

  1. 3Blue1Brown, Neural networks (video series)

    Animated explanations of what a neural network is, how gradient descent trains it and what backpropagation computes.

    Module 1

    • Chapter 1: But what is a neural network?
    • Chapter 2: Gradient descent
    • Chapter 3: What is backpropagation really doing?
    • Chapter 4: Backpropagation calculus
  2. Andrej Karpathy, Neural Networks: Zero to Hero (YouTube)

    A video course that writes neural networks in code from scratch, from backpropagation up to a small GPT.

    Modules 1, 2

    • Building micrograd (backpropagation)
    • Building makemore (language modeling and its loss)
    • Let's build GPT
    • Let's build the GPT tokenizer
  3. OpenAI, Spinning Up in Deep RL

    A short, practical introduction to reinforcement learning concepts and policy gradient algorithms, including PPO.

    Modules 3, 5

    • Part 1: Key concepts in RL
    • Part 3: Intro to policy optimization
    • Proximal Policy Optimization
  4. Richard Sutton and Andrew Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction (2nd edition, free online)

    The standard reinforcement learning textbook. Read chapters 1 to 6 for the foundations, then chapter 13 for policy gradient methods, including REINFORCE in section 13.3.

    Module 3

    • Full book as a PDF
  5. Nathan Lambert, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (rlhfbook.com)

    A free online book on RLHF and post-training for language models: reward models, policy gradients, direct alignment, verifiable rewards and over-optimization.

    Modules 4, 5, 6, 7, 8

    • Chapter 5: Reward Modeling
    • Chapter 6: Reinforcement Learning
    • Chapter 7: Reasoning and Inference-Time Scaling
    • Chapter 8: Direct-Alignment Algorithms
    • Chapter 14: Over-Optimization
    • Chapter 15: Regularization
    • Appendix B: Beyond “Just Style”
  6. Ouyang et al. (2022), Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (InstructGPT)

    Sets out the three-stage recipe of supervised fine-tuning, a reward model and PPO, and the loss and objective used in Modules 4 and 5.

    Modules 4, 5

  7. Schulman et al. (2017), Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms

    Introduces PPO and its clipped objective, the RL algorithm behind most RLHF.

    Module 5

  8. Rafailov et al. (2023), Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model

    Solves the RLHF objective in closed form and turns it into a single loss on preference pairs, with no reward model or RL loop.

    Modules 4, 5, 6

  9. Gao, Schulman and Hilton (2022), Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization

    Measures how true quality first rises and then falls as a policy is optimized against a learned reward model, and how this scales.

    Module 8

Also cited in the modules

These sources back up specific claims in Modules 4, 7 and 8.

  • Lambert et al. (2024), Tülu 3: Pushing Frontiers in Open Language Model Post-Training

    Names and defines RLVR.

    Module 7

  • Shao et al. (2024), DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models

    Introduces GRPO.

    Module 7

  • Singhal et al. (2023), A Long Way to Go: Investigating Length Correlations in RLHF

    Shows how much of RLHF's reward gain comes from longer responses.

    Module 8

  • Sharma et al. (2023), Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models

    Links sycophancy to human preference judgments.

    Module 8

  • Bradley and Terry (1952), Rank Analysis of Incomplete Block Designs: I. The Method of Paired Comparisons

    The original Bradley-Terry model (Biometrika). Behind a paywall in most places.

    Module 4
