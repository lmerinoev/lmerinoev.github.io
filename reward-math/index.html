How language models are trained on human preferences

Eight modules, in order, from the basics of neural networks to reward hacking. Each one explains an idea in plain words, gives the formulas with every symbol defined, works a small example by hand, and ends with exercises. Each term is defined before it is used, and the glossary links back to where.

Formulas use five colors, always with the same meaning: green for the preferred response, red for the rejected one, blue for the model being trained, violet for fixed functions such as σ and log, and amber for the reference model and penalty terms.

Each module ends with links to the matching parts of the resources, which are listed there in a suggested reading order.

  1. 1Neural network basics

    Parameters, loss functions, gradients, backpropagation and gradient descent, explained with one neuron.

    {\theta} \leftarrow {\theta} - \eta\,\nabla_{{\theta}}\mathcal{L}({\theta})
  2. 2Pretraining

    Next-token prediction, softmax and the cross-entropy loss that pretraining minimizes.

    \mathcal{L}({\theta}) = -\textstyle\sum_t {\log}\,p_{{\theta}}(x_t \mid x_{<t})
  3. 3Reinforcement learning basics

    Agents, policies, rewards, expected return and the REINFORCE policy gradient.

    \nabla_{{\theta}} J = \mathbb{E}\big[\textstyle\sum_t \nabla_{{\theta}}{\log}\,{\pi_\theta}(a_t\mid s_t)\,R(\tau)\big]
  4. 4Reward models

    Turning “A is better than B” judgments into a scoring function with the Bradley-Terry model.

    P({y_w}\succ {y_l}) = {\sigma}\big({r_\phi}(x,{y_w}) - {r_\phi}(x,{y_l})\big)
  5. 5RLHF policy optimization

    The RLHF objective, what the KL penalty does, and how PPO optimizes it.

    \max_{{\theta}}\ \mathbb{E}[r_\phi(x,y)] - {\beta}\,\mathrm{KL}({\pi_\theta}\,\|\,{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}})
  6. 6DPO

    Direct preference optimization: solving the RLHF objective on paper and training on preferences with no separate reward model.

    {\beta}\,{\log}\dfrac{{\pi_\theta}(y\mid x)}{{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(y\mid x)}
  7. 7Verifiable rewards

    Binary rewards for math and code: 1 when the answer is right or the tests pass, otherwise 0.

    r(x,y) = \mathbb{1}[\,\text{tests pass}\,]
  8. 8Reward hacking and overoptimization

    Why optimizing a learned reward amplifies whatever raters favored, and how that produces recognizable habits in model writing.

    d = \sqrt{\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}({\pi}\,\|\,{\pi_{\mathrm{init}}})}
