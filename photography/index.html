A deck of self-assigned challenges for street and candid photography. Pick one, go shoot, come back for the next. Nothing gets posted here — the pictures stay in your pocket; the practice is the point. Take one for yourself or send one to a friend.

The Masters

Four photographers this deck steals from. When a challenge feels impossible, look at how they did it.

Joel Meyerowitz
New York · b. 1938

Made color street photography legitimate when everyone said it couldn’t be art. Stood on Fifth Avenue for years. His lesson: the street is a stage — pick your spot, wait, and the picture assembles itself around you.

Deck cards: Work the Corner · Follow the Light · Three Planes
Daidō Moriyama
Tokyo · b. 1938

Are, bure, boke — rough, blurred, out of focus. Shoots like a stray dog moves through the city: fast, low, on instinct, without preciousness. His lesson: grain and blur are texture, not failure.

Deck cards: Are, Bure, Boke · After Dark
Vivian Maier
Chicago · 1926–2009

A nanny who shot 150,000 frames nobody saw until after her death. Rolleiflex at the waist, eyes on the street. Her lesson: be invisible in plain sight — waiting rooms, reflections, quiet juxtapositions.

Deck cards: Two Worlds · The Theatre of Waiting · Waist Level
Samuel Lintaro Hopf
Hamburg · samuel streetlife

Works the street openly and calmly, camera visible, no sneaking. Finds one good scene and stays with it. His lesson: thirty frames from one spot beat three hundred from nowhere.

Deck cards: One Scene, Thirty Frames · Carry the Big Camera

The Kit

Each challenge suggests a camera. The tool shapes the picture — that’s why it’s part of the assignment.

Ricoh GR II
The pocket knife. 28mm, snap focus, invisible. Default for the courage cards.
Fujifilm X100V
The daily driver. One focal length, optical finder, leaf-shutter quiet. For patient work.
Fujifilm X-M1
The slow one. Deliberate walks and color studies with classic Fuji files.
Digicams
The toys. CCD colors, tiny sensors, slow AF. Permission to stop being precious.
Nikon DSLR
The loud one. Take it out when the assignment is to be seen, not to hide.
